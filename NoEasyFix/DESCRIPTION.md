# No Easy Fix — player description

## Short summary

No Easy Fix treats a standard BattleMech as a finished machine. You can still repair it, change its armor, and swap gear that fits the space the stock loadout already used — but you cannot hang extra weapons or equipment in leftover empty slots. That leftover space is for OmniMechs. Changing a location to a different *kind* of weapon or gear is an expensive refit; repairs and like-for-like swaps stay at normal cost.

## Overview

HBS BattleTech lets you fill every empty inventory box a chassis has, so a “stock” Centurion or Cataphract can grow a lot of extra guns once you own it. No Easy Fix puts the factory layout back in charge for ordinary BattleMechs, and leaves OmniMechs as the machines that can still use leftover space.

**On a standard BattleMech**, each location may only use as much inventory space as that variant already used in its published stock loadout. You may pull a weapon and put something else in that location if it fits the same budget. Leftover empty boxes still appear on the silhouette — they are just locked. You cannot add a small laser to an unused support hardpoint, hang gear on an empty arm, or stuff extra heat sinks into space the stock kit never used.

**What you can still do on a standard ’Mech**

- Repair damaged or destroyed components, structure, and armor at normal prices.
- Strip and rebuild, so long as you do not go past that location’s stock budget.
- Swap like for like: any Medium Laser for a Medium Laser, any AC/10 for another AC/10, ammo for the same kind of ammo.
- Fit a smaller item into a larger stock space. The unused remainder stays locked.
- Use heat banks, thermal exchangers, and similar heat gear in locations that already had heat sinks, if they fit that heat-sink space.
- Fit targeting computers, TTS, C3, ECM, and other electronics/mods without the leftover lock. Those are not treated as extra guns in empty structure.

**What you cannot do on a standard ’Mech**

- Add extra weapons, ammo, jump jets, or heat sinks into leftover empty slots.
- Turn an unused hardpoint into a new mount unless that location still has unused *stock* budget — which it will not, if that hardpoint was empty at the factory.

**OmniMechs** are unchanged by the lock. If the chassis is an Omni, leftover space still works, and Omni-style installs stay cheap.

**Refit cost.** Repairs never get more expensive. Swapping in the same kind of equipment stays at the usual install price. Installing a *different* kind of thing in that location — a pulse laser where a standard laser sat, an LB 10-X where an AC/10 sat, a missile on an arm that never carried one — is a type-change refit. Those jobs cost more C-bills and take longer in the shop. Moving a component to another location is charged as an install *there*: if that location never had that kind of gear, it is the expensive refit, not a free shuffle.

**The MechLab.** Stock gear is hinted with tinted ghosts that match the game’s own weapon colours. Locked leftover tiles show a short bar. Each location label shows how much of the stock budget is in use. F8 (or the stock-view button) fades the live kit so you can read the factory layout underneath.

**Saves.** A ’Mech you already customized keeps what it has; it cannot grow *further* past that. New machines from storage, salvage, or assembly use the published stock layout as their budget.

This mod does not turn OmniMechs into free-form pod ’Mechs, does not rewrite chassis files, and does not fill empty crits with dummy equipment. It only changes what the MechLab will accept on a standard BattleMech, and what a type-change refit costs.
